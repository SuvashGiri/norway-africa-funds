Hard Currency, Local Finance

What contract currency can and cannot tell us about Norfund's African portfolio.

Suvash Giri · November 2026 · What one US dollar cost in Zambian kwacha

Independent project, built with an AI assistant from public data. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Norfund or NBIM.

Conclusion: Norfund’s public portfolio data record African commitments outside South Africa predominantly in hard currency, but the Ghana example shows that contract denomination alone does not describe the financing reaching local businesses. The development question is how financing terms help viable enterprises withstand currency shocks, sustain jobs and expand access to finance. Currency choice must be assessed alongside pricing, tenor and repayment capacity, while patient capital or risk-sharing may provide additionality where comparable commercial financing is unavailable.

Where could a dollar loan have broken?

A business that earns local currency and repays in dollars needs more local currency each time its currency falls. If its cash covers debt service 1.5 times (DSCR 1.5), it can absorb a fall of about 33% before the cash runs out, assuming no hedge and unchanged local-currency cash flow. At DSCR 1.3, about 23%. The rule is 1 − 1/DSCR, the same logic as the Zambia calculator below.

Zambia shows why the end point misleads. At the latest rate in this note (18 June 2026), the kwacha is only 3% weaker than in November 2021. In March 2025 it was 40% weaker. For a hypothetical unhedged borrower with DSCR 1.5 and unchanged kwacha income, a dollar payment due that month could not have been covered from cash flow, even though the start and end points look almost flat.

Two local-currency structures

GIP Ghana

Norfund invests USD equity in a platform that lends to Ghanaian SMEs in cedi. Cedi-denominated loans avoid imposing a USD debt-service obligation on locally earning SMEs; the platform's own currency exposure and its management are not established by these sources.

Sun King, Kenya

A Kenyan-shilling securitisation of solar pay-as-you-go receivables. Commercial banks hold the senior tranche; Norfund, BII and FMO hold the mezzanine. Customer payments and the notes are both in shillings.

Implication, under the default assumptions. Local-currency debt removes the direct exchange-rate sensitivity of repayments, but its higher interest rate leaves little cash-flow headroom (DSCR 1.12). The financing challenge is therefore not currency alone: tenor, pricing and repayment structure must also fit the business’s capacity to pay. This is where patient capital, or terms unavailable commercially, may provide additionality.

Assumptions: both rates fixed for three years; cash available for debt service constant; fees and hedging costs excluded. Currency-matched repayments do not make the business’s own cash flows predictable. Annual annuity payments; DSCR = cash available for debt service ÷ annual payment. “Thin” marks DSCR below 1.2, an illustrative threshold, not an actual lender covenant. Actual rate differences can reflect inflation, currency expectations, credit risk and liquidity; the rates here are illustrative. Depreciation can be gradual or abrupt, and real loans may be hedged or partly USD-linked.

Method and limits

  • Norfund: active commitments from the investment overview workbook (file dated 30.09.2026; FX reporting date 30.06.2026), allocated by Norfund's country-of-operation split and resolved at instrument level. Commitments are not market values.
  • Contract currency is not economic exposure. Investees may earn in USD, hedge, or on-lend in local currency; the workbook shows none of this.
  • The Zambia calculation is illustrative and uses assumed rates.
  • Currency falls: start rates 1–4 Nov 2021 and weakest-day rates from ValutaFX; latest rates from Pound Sterling Live (NGN 27.10.2026, XOF 23.10.2026, KES and ZAR 11.11.2026) and ValutaFX (GHS 18.05.2026, TZS 26.05.2026, UGX 12.06.2026, ZMW 18.06.2026). Daily reference rates, not the rate a borrower actually paid. MZN and CDF were not available from these sources.
  • Country names were normalised before aggregation. An earlier draft dropped DR Congo because of a trailing space in the source.
